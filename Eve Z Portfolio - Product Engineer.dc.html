Deployed operational workflow

Detector Data Quality Control

Traffic engineers must reconcile detector volumes, identifiers, dictionary mappings, movements, and model baselines — where missing data and incorrect mappings quietly produce misleading conclusions. I translated that engineer-led review into a browser-based QC workflow. Review time fell from roughly 2.5 hours to 10 minutes per intersection.

Agentic BI on transportation network data: deterministic QC feeds a tool-using agent with RAG and structured outputs, an automated judge screens every draft for faithfulness and customer-readiness, and an engineer signs off before export. 1,522 intersections in production QC scope; agentic layer under evaluation.

Deployed operational tool

Proactive Detour Retiming

When a road closes, displaced traffic floods detour routes whose signals were timed for normal conditions — and agencies usually learn about it from complaints, not data. This tool recomputes detour volumes during a closure and translates them into the timing adjustment each intersection needs, so signals are retimed before the queues form.

research direction

Designing AI-Assisted Workplaces

The people who understand a workflow best are often not the people who design its software. Experienced professionals must therefore adapt their work to rigid systems, move manually among disconnected platforms, and rely on spreadsheets or informal procedures to fill the gaps.

I am interested in AI-supported end-user development: enabling domain professionals to create and customize workplace tools without becoming traditional software engineers. Generative AI could help workers translate situated expertise into prompts, rules, examples, and reusable workflows. Designers, engineers, and IT teams would continue to provide secure infrastructure, evaluation mechanisms, shared standards, and organizational governance.

My central research question: How can domain professionals create and continually improve AI-assisted workplace tools while organizations preserve security, interoperability, provenance, consistency, and accountability?

01

Professionals as Toolmakers

How can experienced workers translate tacit knowledge into customizable, prompt-generated tools? How should interfaces help nonprogrammers inspect, test, and correct what AI creates?

02

Coordinated Access Across Fragmented Systems

How can an interface bring authorized information from disconnected systems into a coordinated workspace without indiscriminately centralizing sensitive data?

03

Verifiable Human–AI Collaboration

How should AI retrieve, compare, and draft while making evidence, uncertainty, and limitations visible — and preserving human authority over consequential decisions?

Autonomy principle

The system may monitor, retrieve, compare, and draft. The professional verifies, corrects, approves, and acts.

product instinct

From Frontline Problems to Product Direction

Although I do not hold a formal product-management title, I continuously identify overlooked users, unmet workflow needs, and opportunities to improve existing platforms. I translate frontline operational problems into product concepts, write proposals, and collaborate with product managers, technical leads, and organizational leadership to examine feasibility and adoption. This experience has taught me to connect four perspectives: what users experience in practice, what the organization permits, what technology can reliably support, and what can become a sustainable shared capability.

about me

How I got here.

I came to operational engineering through design and then through quantitative research — an unusual route that turned out to be the useful one. Landscape architecture at Beijing Forestry University taught me to read a system as relationships among people, institutions, and infrastructure. Graduate work in urban spatial analytics taught me to test claims against data instead of accepting them.

Transportation operations then put me next to the people doing the work: engineers reconciling detector data across a large signal network, operators piecing together an incident from six systems that do not talk to each other. Watching skilled professionals absorb the friction their software created is what moved me from analysis toward building, and from building toward research.

B.E. in Landscape Architecture from Beijing Forestry University (Outstanding Graduate) and an M.S. in Urban Spatial Analytics from the University of Pennsylvania; finalist, 2023 MetroLab Student Cup; SMACH 2021 Construction Award.
